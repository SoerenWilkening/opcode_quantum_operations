## 1. Julia source excerpt

`third_party/bennett/src/multiplier.jl` lines 1–33, verbatim:

```julia
"""Shift-and-add multiplier: result = a * b  (mod 2^W)."""
function lower_mul!(gates::Vector{ReversibleGate}, wa::WireAllocator,
                    a::Vector{Int}, b::Vector{Int}, W::Int)
    return lower_mul_wide!(gates, wa, a, b, W, W)
end

"""
Shift-and-add widening multiplier: result = a * b with `result_width` bits.

When result_width == W: standard mod 2^W multiplication.
When result_width == 2W: full product without truncation.
"""
function lower_mul_wide!(gates::Vector{ReversibleGate}, wa::WireAllocator,
                         a::Vector{Int}, b::Vector{Int}, W::Int, result_width::Int)
    accum = allocate!(wa, result_width)
    # Bennett-5kio / U109: each outer iter pushes ≤W Toffolis (partial-prod
    # AND-tree) + lower_add! (~5*result_width gates). Total upper bound:
    # W*(W + 5*result_width). Avoids O(log²) reallocations as the gate
    # vector grows from ~0 to multi-thousand on Int32+ multiplies.
    sizehint!(gates, length(gates) + W * (W + 5 * result_width))
    for i in 1:W
        shift = i - 1
        pp = allocate!(wa, result_width)
        for k in 1:W
            dest = k + shift
            dest > result_width && break
            push!(gates, ToffoliGate(a[k], b[i], pp[dest]))
        end
        new_accum = lower_add!(gates, wa, accum, pp, result_width)
        accum = new_accum
    end
    return accum
end
```

The whole cost is dominated by the inner adder, which is the one slot where libcqops departs from the source above. **The adder we port into that slot is `lower_add_cuccaro!` (K08), not the `lower_add!` that multiplier.jl:29 calls** — see §2 for the decision and §5 delta 4 for the accepted cost. `lower_add_cuccaro!` is excerpted verbatim in [`K08.md`](K08.md) §1 rather than duplicated here; its counts, re-verified against K08.md §3's `push!`-site table on 2026-08-14, are `CNOT = 4W−2`, `Toffoli = 2W−3`, `NOT = 0`, `total = 6W−5`, valid for `W ≥ 2` (adder.jl:102–143 for `W ≥ 3`, adder.jl:84–96 for the `W = 2` branch, docstring at adder.jl:35–38). At `W ≤ 1` it delegates to `lower_add!` (adder.jl:66) and is **no longer in-place** — hence K11's `W = 1` special case in §3.

The displaced ripple adder is retained below because **Appendix A** — the upstream cross-check — is built on it. `third_party/bennett/src/adder.jl` lines 1–18, verbatim:

```julia
"""Ripple-carry full adder: result = a + b  (mod 2^W)."""
function lower_add!(gates::Vector{ReversibleGate}, wa::WireAllocator,
                    a::Vector{Int}, b::Vector{Int}, W::Int)
    result = allocate!(wa, W)
    carry  = allocate!(wa, W)
    # Bennett-5kio / U109: 3 CNOTs per i + 2 Toffolis for i<W = 5W - 2 gates.
    sizehint!(gates, length(gates) + 5 * W)
    for i in 1:W
        push!(gates, CNOTGate(a[i], result[i]))
        push!(gates, CNOTGate(b[i], result[i]))
        if i < W
            push!(gates, ToffoliGate(a[i], b[i], carry[i + 1]))
            push!(gates, ToffoliGate(result[i], carry[i], carry[i + 1]))
        end
        push!(gates, CNOTGate(carry[i], result[i]))
    end
    return result
end
```

Its counts, re-derived here from the `push!` sites at adder.jl:9, 10, 12, 13, 15, are `CNOT = 3W`, `Toffoli = 2(W−1)`, `NOT = 0`, `total = 5W−2` — matching the sizehint comment at adder.jl:6. **These feed Appendix A only. They are not K11's pinned numbers.**

## 2. Which strategy, and what happens to the high half

### Two strategies exist upstream

Bennett ships two multipliers and dispatches between them in `src/lowering/arith.jl`:

| Strategy | Source | Selected by | Cost profile (Bennett's own words) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `:shift_add` | `multiplier.jl` `lower_mul!` | `_pick_mul_strategy(:auto, W)` with default `target=:gate_count` (arith.jl:50–57); used at arith.jl:252 | "Wins on total Toffoli count and wire budget at every supported width" (arith.jl:35–36) |
| `:qcla_tree` | `mul_qcla_tree.jl` `lower_mul_qcla_tree!` (Sun–Borissov 2026, arXiv:2604.09847 — mul_qcla_tree.jl:4; arith.jl:37 mis-dates it "2023") | explicit `mul=:qcla_tree` only. The `target === :depth` arm exists (arith.jl:55) but is **unreachable from the binop dispatcher**: arith.jl:217 calls `_pick_mul_strategy(mul, W)` without threading `target`, and that is the only production call site. Used at arith.jl:244 | `O(log²n)` Toffoli depth, but "~5× more total Toffoli and ~2.5× more wires" (arith.jl:38–39) |

(A third, Karatsuba, was **removed** on 2026-04-27 — multiplier.jl:35–41 records the empirical sweep: 1.91–3.49× *worse* Toffoli than schoolbook at every `W ≤ 64`, crossover past `W = 128`.)

### libcqops v1 ports the `:shift_add` *skeleton*, i.e. `lower_mul!`

Three independent reasons, in priority order:

1. **`:qcla_tree` cannot produce a truncated product without leaking a dirty rail.** `lower_mul_qcla_tree!` returns `2W` wires; the truncating caller slices `full[1:W]` (arith.jl:249). Bennett's own comment at arith.jl:222–224 says this "keeps the low half (mod 2^W) and **STRANDS the high W as dirty ancillae**". In libcqops that is precisely the Rule 6 / PRD §10 hard error — a `cqrt_free` on a non-provably-zero rail — and per NORTH_STAR §3 a silent miscompile if the assert were ever relaxed. Disqualifying.
2. **Qubits, not depth, are the scarce resource here.** `:qcla_tree` costs ~2.5× the wires and ~5× the Toffolis (⇒ ~5× the T-count) to buy depth we are not being asked for. PRD §1's constraints say nothing about circuit depth.
3. **`:shift_add` is the one whose gate counts can be cross-checked against a published Bennett artifact** — see **Appendix A**, residual zero on all six columns at two widths. Note carefully what this reason does and does not buy after the inner-adder substitution below: it validates the *skeleton* (the partial-product loop, the truncation, the `2×compute + W` wrap arithmetic), not K11's final numbers.

### STRATEGY DECISION 2026-08-14 — the inner adder is **K8 Cuccaro**, not ripple

**This is the one decision that used to be open in this document, and it is now closed.**
Bennett's `lower_mul_wide!` calls `lower_add!` — the out-of-place ripple adder — at
multiplier.jl:29; the string `cuccaro` does not occur in `multiplier.jl` at all. libcqops
substitutes `lower_add_cuccaro!` (K08) into that slot. **This is a deliberate delta from
upstream, recorded as §5 delta 4 and in PRD-v1.md:443–453, not a port.**

Four reasons, and one objection that does not apply:

1. **Scratch collapses.** `3W² + W` → `W² + 2W`: **3104 → 1088 qubits at `W = 32`**, a
   2.85× reduction. Ripple allocates three fresh `W`-bit registers per outer iteration
   (`pp` at multiplier.jl:23, plus `result` and `carry` inside `lower_add!` at
   adder.jl:4–5); Cuccaro allocates `pp` plus one ancilla. Under D2 the pool ceiling is
   set to `qec_n_logical` when the QEC sink is active, so this is the difference between
   fitting and failing loud.
2. **Toffolis drop too**, `5W² − 3W` → `5W² − 5W` sandwiched: `2W` fewer, at every `W`.
   Under a surface-code cost model Toffolis are the expensive gates.
3. **K8 exists in the catalogue for exactly this.** PRD-v1.md:429 lists K8 as the
   in-place accumulator; PRD-v1.md:366–367 says in as many words that "Cuccaro is still
   needed — as the *in-place* accumulator inside the multiplier". If K11 used ripple, K8
   would have **no consumer in v1** and Step 15 would need re-justifying.
4. **I6(a) is satisfied, and more comfortably than with ripple.** Cuccaro's operands here
   are `accum` and `pp[j]` — both scratch — so K08's `i6_ok = false` finding (it writes
   into its *addend* register: adder.jl:104, 109, 110, 117, 130, 135, 136, 141) is
   harmless: those targets are scratch too. See §2b.

**The Rule 7 objection to Cuccaro does not reach K11.** Cuccaro is barred from K6/K7
because its uncompute is the *reverse circuit* rather than a re-run, which does not match
CQ_lang's `_unc` "recompute from the still-live sources" contract. Inside K11 the
accumulator is **internal to the kernel**: it is created, used and destroyed between one
`cqrt_mul_<W>` entry and its return, and it is never exposed to `_unc`. CQ_lang's `_unc`
re-enters `cq_mul` from the top, which re-runs the whole sandwich — `f ⊕ f = 0` — and
never asks the accumulator to reverse itself.

**The price, stated plainly:** the composition "shift-add over Cuccaro" exists nowhere in
Bennett, is exercised by no Bennett test, and its L4 golden therefore **cannot be
cross-checked against any upstream artifact**. K11's golden is self-pinned. That is the
accepted cost, recorded as §5 delta 4, and it is the sole reason Appendix A survives.

### The high half: it is never computed, not sliced off

CQ_lang's `mul` is same-width. `third_party/cq_lang/opcode_table.yaml:186` declares `mul` in `family: int_arith`, `widths: [i1,i8,i16,i32,i64,i128]`, `llvm_inst: "llvm::Instruction::Mul"` — and LLVM `mul` has matching operand and result widths (Bennett states this explicitly at arith.jl:227–232, Bennett-jpa5). There is **no widening-multiply opcode** in the table: no `mul_wide`, no `umulh`/`smulh`.

So libcqops instantiates Bennett's own truncating entry point, `lower_mul!` = `lower_mul_wide!(…, W, W)` (multiplier.jl:4), i.e. `result_width = W`. At `result_width = W` the high half **is never materialised at all**:

- multiplier.jl:26 — `dest > result_width && break` drops every partial-product Toffoli of weight ≥ `W`. Those `W(W−1)/2` AND terms are never emitted, so no qubit is ever allocated for them.
- The accumulator emits no carry-out either, so the `2^W` overflow bit does not exist. For **Cuccaro** this is explicit in the docstring: adder.jl:33–34 says the pinned counts are for "mod 2^W output — no carry-out emitted", and adder.jl:41–42 notes the original paper's `2n` NOTs "appear in the carry-out variant; this mod-2^W form omits both". (For the ripple appendix the same follows from adder.jl:11 — `if i < W` writes `carry[2..W]` only.)

**Therefore K11 has no high half to leak.** Truncation is structural, not a slice. This is the decisive contrast with `:qcla_tree`, where truncation *is* a slice and the discarded half *is* a dirty ancilla.

What *is* dirty at the end of the compute half is the scratch — the single in-place `accum`, the `W` partial-product registers `pp[j]`, and the Cuccaro ancillae — and that is exactly what the sandwich's reverse half returns to `|0⟩` before `cq_scratch_free`. See §4.

## 2b. Gate sequence as indexed pseudo-code

libcqops vocabulary. `a`, `b` are the kernel's `const cq_bit *` sources (`W` bits each); `dst` is the caller's rail; everything else is scratch.

Scratch layout, allocated up front by the kernel and **pre-materialised to `CQ_BIT_Q` at
step 0 by `cq_sandwich`** (I6(b); every bit is born `CQ_BIT_ZERO`, so materialisation
draws `W² + 2W` qubits from the pool and emits **zero gates**):

```
scratch.accum           : W bits           -- ONE register, updated IN PLACE
scratch.pp[j]           : W bits, j=0..W-1 -- W registers, never reused
scratch.x[j]            : 1 bit,  j=0..W-1 -- Cuccaro's ancilla, one per call
```

There is no `accum` chain and no `sum`/`carry` registers: Cuccaro writes the sum back
into its own second operand (K08.md §2: libcqops' `acc` is Bennett's `b`), and uses the
addend's wires as transient carry storage. `pp[j]` **cannot** be recycled for `j+1`:
Cuccaro's contract restores its addend (adder.jl:50–51), so `pp[j]` still holds the
partial product afterwards. Clearing it would mean re-emitting the phase-P Toffolis —
that is the pp-recycling variant in §5 note 8, not adopted in v1.

`x[j]` is **passed in by K11**, not allocated inside K8 as Bennett does at adder.jl:69 —
see the interface note under I6(b) below.

The compute half has **exactly** `n_compute = W(W+1)/2 + W(6W−5) = (13W² − 9W)/2` steps —
one gate *slot* per step. At `W = 8` that is **380**. Do **not** size the step loop from
Bennett's `sizehint!` at multiplier.jl:20 (`W(W + 5·result_width)` = 384 at `W = 8`); it
is an upper bound for a different adder. The step function is naturally two-level, so
index it as `s = (j, phase)` with `j ∈ [0,W)` the outer shift index. Written flat:

```
/* ---------- step 0 (driver): pre-materialise ALL of scratch  [I6(b)] ----------
   accum, pp[0..W-1], x[0..W-1]  ->  every bit CQ_BIT_Q
   W^2 + 2W qubits drawn from the pool; ZERO gates emitted.               */

/* ---------- compute half: step(s) for s in [0, n_compute) ---------- */

for j in 0 .. W-1:                       /* outer: multiplier bit b[j] */

    /* --- phase P: partial product, weight-shifted by j --- */
    for k in 0 .. W-1:
        d = k + j
        if d >= W: break                 /* multiplier.jl:26 — truncation */
        CCX( a[k], b[j] -> scratch.pp[j][d] )

    /* --- phase A: cq_addacc( accum, pp[j] )  ==  K08 Cuccaro, 6W-5 gates ---
       Bennett arg order is lower_add_cuccaro!(a = pp[j], b = accum):
       the ADDEND is pp[j] and the SUM lands in accum.  Both are scratch,
       which is what makes K08's i6_ok=false harmless here.  Gate-by-gate
       sequence: K08.md §2 (W>=3 at adder.jl:102-143, W==2 at adder.jl:84-96). */
    cq_addacc( scratch.accum, scratch.pp[j], scratch.x[j], W )

/* ---------- copy-out: W steps ---------- */
for i in 0 .. W-1:
    CX( scratch.accum[i] -> dst[i] )             /* this is the "dst ^= f(a,b)" */

/* ---------- reverse half ---------- */
cq_sandwich re-runs step(s) for s = n_compute-1 down to 0.
```

Note `scratch.pp[j][0..j-1]` are never targeted by phase P — that is what encodes the
shift — so they hold the *value* zero for the whole compute half. Under I6(b) they are
nonetheless `CQ_BIT_Q` qubits holding `|0⟩`, and every Cuccaro gate that touches them is
emitted physically. That is a real, deliberate cost; see §3's note on the forgone
first-iteration folding.

### I6(a) — every compute-half gate target is scratch

**Holds.** Every target in the Cuccaro composition is a scratch bit:

| Gate | Controls | Target | Target in scratch? |
|---|---|---|---|
| phase-P `CCX` | `a[k]`, `b[j]` (**the only** kernel sources touched) | `pp[j][d]` | ✔ |
| Cuccaro MAJ/UMA `CX`/`CCX` into the addend (adder.jl:104, 109, 110, 117, 130, 135, 136, 141) | `x[j]`, `accum[·]`, `pp[j][·]` | `pp[j][·]` | ✔ |
| Cuccaro `CX`/`CCX` into the accumulator (adder.jl:102, 108, 116, 123, 124, 125, 131, 137, 143) | `pp[j][·]`, `x[j]`, `accum[·]` | `accum[·]` | ✔ |
| Cuccaro ancilla `CX` (adder.jl:103, 142) | `pp[j][0]` | `x[j]` | ✔ |

`a` and `b` appear **only as controls**, only in phase P, and are never written — so the
kernel's Rule 7 contract (`a`, `b` unchanged) holds gate-by-gate, not merely end-to-end.

**This is the point at which K08's `i6_ok = false` becomes harmless.** K08.md §2 records
that Cuccaro targets its own *addend* register, which is why its prototype must take that
addend **non-`const`** (K08.md D4: `void cq_addacc(cq_ctx*, cq_bit *acc, cq_bit *b, int W)`).
That deletes the by-construction half of the I6 defence *for K8*. Inside K11 the addend is
`pp[j]` — scratch — so the Debug scratch-extent assertion passes and I6(a) is intact.
**M18 must therefore never wire the kernel's real `a` or `b` operand into K8's addend
slot**: that single line would be an I6(a) violation, a source mutation and a Rule 7
contract break simultaneously. Assert it in Debug.

### I6(b) — every scratch bit is `CQ_BIT_Q` for the whole compute half

**Guaranteed by the driver, not by this construction.** `cq_sandwich` pre-materialises
`accum`, all `pp[j]` and all `x[j]` at step 0 (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md §0.2). **K11 needs
this — the adopted Cuccaro composition is exposed to R8, and here is the witness**, traced
against the source rather than asserted. Take `j = 1` at any `W ≥ 4` (so Cuccaro's middle-MAJ
loop `for i in 2:(W-2)` at adder.jl:107 is non-empty), with `a[0]` and `b[1]` both
`CQ_BIT_Q`. Recall the call is `lower_add_cuccaro!(a = pp[1], b = accum)`, and that
`pp[1][0]` is **never** written by phase P — `d = k + j ≥ 1` for `j ≥ 1`, which is exactly
what encodes the shift — so it is still `BIT_ZERO` when Cuccaro starts:

```
forward  adder.jl:102  CX (pp[1][0]=ZERO -> accum[0])       control ZERO ->  0 gates
         adder.jl:103  CX (pp[1][0]=ZERO -> x[1]=ZERO)      control ZERO ->  0 gates
         adder.jl:104  CCX(x[1]=ZERO, accum[0] -> pp[1][0]) control ZERO ->  0 gates
         adder.jl:108  CX (pp[1][1]=Q -> accum[1])                           1 gate
         adder.jl:109  CX (pp[1][1]=Q -> pp[1][0]=ZERO)
                            -> MATERIALISE pp[1][0] := Q                     1 gate   <== !!
         ... middle MAJs, phase 2, middle UMAs — every operand now Q ...
         adder.jl:141  CCX(x[1]=ZERO, accum[0] -> pp[1][0]=Q) ctrl ZERO ->   0 gates
         adder.jl:142  CX (pp[1][0]=Q -> x[1]=ZERO)
                            -> MATERIALISE x[1] := Q                         1 gate   <== !!
         adder.jl:143  CX (x[1]=Q -> accum[0])                               1 gate

  END OF FORWARD HALF: pp[1][0] AND x[1] are both CQ_BIT_Q. Kinds are monotone
  (D6, no demotion), so the reverse pass sees Q at every one of these wires.

reverse  adder.jl:143  CX (x[1]=Q -> accum[0])                               1 gate  (cancels)
         adder.jl:142  CX (pp[1][0]=Q -> x[1]=Q)                             1 gate  (cancels)
         adder.jl:141  CCX(x[1]=Q, accum[0] -> pp[1][0]=Q)                   1 gate  <== SPURIOUS
         ... middle UMAs, phase 2, middle MAJs — all cancel ...
         adder.jl:109  CX (pp[1][1]=Q -> pp[1][0]=Q)                         1 gate  (cancels)
         adder.jl:104  CCX(x[1]=Q, accum[0] -> pp[1][0]=Q)                   1 gate  <== SPURIOUS
         adder.jl:103  CX (pp[1][0]=Q -> x[1]=Q)                             1 gate  <== SPURIOUS
         adder.jl:102  CX (pp[1][0]=Q -> accum[0])                           1 gate  <== SPURIOUS
```

**Net divergence: the reverse half emits four gates — 2 `CCX` (:141, :104) and 2 `CX`
(:103, :102) — that the forward half never emitted.**

Two scratch wires are indicted, by the same mechanism, one call apart:

- **`pp[1][0]`** is read as a **control** at adder.jl:102–103 while still `BIT_ZERO`, and
  materialised as a **target** at adder.jl:109 by the first middle MAJ. (At `W = 3` the
  middle-MAJ loop `for i in 2:(W−2)` is empty, but adder.jl:117 —
  `CNOT(a[W−1], a[W−2])` = `CX(pp[1][1] → pp[1][0])` — materialises it in exactly the same
  way, so the exposure is present at every `W ≥ 3`, not only `W ≥ 4`.)
- **`x[1]`** is read as a **control** at adder.jl:104 (and again at :141) while still
  `BIT_ZERO`, and materialised as a **target** at adder.jl:142, the second-to-last gate of
  the call. Note that its *own* write site, adder.jl:103, does **not** materialise it on
  the forward pass — the control there is `pp[1][0]`, still `BIT_ZERO` at that moment. The
  materialisation happens much later and from the *same* wire once that wire has turned
  quantum.

> **⚠️ Do not read the reverse trace as "x[1] is still ZERO on the way back."** An earlier
> draft of this section did, and it is wrong: adder.jl:142 materialises `x[1]` in the
> **forward** half, and D6 forbids demotion, so `x[1]` is `Q` for the whole reverse pass.
> That is what turns :141 and :104 into spurious `CCX`s rather than the 0-gate folds the
> forward emitted. The corrected divergence is 4 gates, not 2.

That is the R8 antecedent exactly, in the construction K11 actually ships, at every width
the ABI uses. It is not inherited from the ripple variant and it does not depend on the
mask search below.

Under I6(b) it cannot happen: `pp[1][0]` and `x[1]` are `CQ_BIT_Q` from step 0, every gate
above emits unconditionally on both passes, and the two halves are identical by
construction.

**Interface consequence for M15/M18 — do not miss this.** Bennett allocates Cuccaro's
ancilla *inside* the function (`X = allocate!(wa, 1)`, adder.jl:69). libcqops **cannot**:
an allocation in the middle of a compute half is a scratch bit that was not pre-materialised
at step 0, which breaks I6(b) for the very wire the trace above indicts. So `x[j]` must be
**owned by K11 and passed in**, inside K11's pre-materialised scratch extent — hence the
extra parameter in the §2b pseudo-code. K08.md D4's prototype
(`cq_addacc(cq_ctx*, cq_bit *acc, cq_bit *b, int W)`) needs a caller-supplied ancilla
argument to be usable inside a sandwich at all. **Settle the signature in M15 before M18
consumes it.**

proposed pre-materialisation as a *mitigation* and left it open. It is now decided

### The R8 finding, condensed — why I6(a) alone was not enough

**Settled 2026-08-14. Retained as the record of how the hazard was found and what the
test suite must still pin.**

The pre-decision justification for replay-in-reverse was: *"scratch is born `BIT_ZERO`, so
materialisation there emits no `X`, and step `s` emits an identical gate sequence forwards
and backwards."* That covers **targets** only. The emitted sequence is a function of the
bit-kinds of *every operand of every gate at the moment it is emitted*, including
**scratch controls** — whose kinds change during the compute half and, because we never
demote (D6), are monotone. On the reverse pass every scratch bit already carries its
**final** kind. The weakest repair that was considered:

> **(I6b-ordering — proposed here, and REJECTED in favour of the plan's I6(b))** In the
> compute half, no scratch bit is read as a **control** at a step that precedes a step
> where that same bit is **materialised**.

Rejected for two reasons: a second mechanism defeats it (below), and it is an ordering
obligation on twelve separate kernels rather than a driver guarantee.

`lower_add!` violates its antecedent: `g4` reads `sum[j][i]` as a control, and `g5` writes `sum[j][i]` immediately afterwards (adder.jl:13 then adder.jl:15). If `sum[j][i]` is still classical at `g4` but `carry[j][i]` is a qubit, then `g5` materialises `sum[j][i]`, and the reverse pass sees a **quantum** control at `g4` where the forward pass saw a classical one.

Worked counterexample on a **standalone `lower_add!`** (K06), `W = 3`, `a = [Q, ZERO, ZERO]`, `b = [Q, ZERO, ZERO]`, applying PRD §3's fold table row by row:

```
forward  i=1: g1 CX(a1=Q -> sum1=ZERO)      -> materialise sum1:=Q,  1 gate
              g2 CX(b1=Q -> sum1=Q)                                   1 gate
              g3 CCX(a1=Q,b1=Q -> carry2=ZERO) -> materialise carry2:=Q, 1 gate
              g4 CCX(sum1=Q, carry1=ZERO -> carry2)  control ZERO ->   0 gates
              g5 CX(carry1=ZERO -> sum1)                               0 gates
forward  i=2: g1,g2  controls ZERO                                     0 gates
              g3 CCX(a2=ZERO,b2=ZERO -> carry3)                        0 gates
              g4 CCX(sum2=ZERO, carry2=Q -> carry3)  control ZERO ->   0 gates   <== !!
              g5 CX(carry2=Q -> sum2=ZERO) -> materialise sum2:=Q,     1 gate
...
reverse  i=2: g5 CX(carry2=Q -> sum2=Q)                                1 gate  (cancels)
              g4 CCX(sum2=Q, carry2=Q -> carry3=ZERO)
                    -> both controls Q, target constant -> MATERIALISE carry3, emit CCX
                                                                       1 gate  <== SPURIOUS
```

The reverse half emits a `CCX` the forward half never emitted, leaving `carry3` holding `sum2·carry2` with an *unknown* shadow. Consequences:

- **L1 is GREEN.** `dst` was copied out before the reverse half; its value is correct.
- **L3 FAILS** — and `cq_scratch_free` hits a non-zero rail, which is a Rule 6 **hard error**, so it fails loud rather than silently. That is the designed-for outcome, but the circuit is still wrong.
- This is exactly the "clean-trace dirty-ancilla" class CLAUDE.md opens with, and it is *only* caught by running L3 over **mixed-kind operand masks**, never over all-quantum or all-classical ones.

**Scope. K11 IS exposed — established twice, on two different constructions.**

*Structurally, for the adopted Cuccaro composition:* see the two bullets under I6(b)
above. `x[j]` and `pp[j][0..j−1]` are read as `BIT_ZERO` controls inside Cuccaro's MAJ
chain and can be materialised later in the same compute half. No mask search is needed —
the exposure follows from the wiring.

*By exhaustive mask search, for the **ripple** composition (Appendix A):* the witnesses
below were produced against the exact loop structure of multiplier.jl:21–31 + adder.jl:8–16.
**⚠️ They are witnesses for a construction K11 no longer uses.** They are retained because
the plan's R8 entry (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md:435) and K06/K07 cite them, and because they
are the only *quantified* evidence anywhere in this catalogue. **They have NOT been
re-derived for the Cuccaro composition** — treat the specific masks and counts as
ripple-only.

| Witness | `a` | `b` | forward gates | reverse gates | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `W = 5`, `{ZERO, Q}` only | `[Q,Z,Q,Z,Z]` | `[Q,Z,Q,Z,Z]` | 22 | 23 | I6b exactly: `g4` reads `sum[2][3]` as a control one step before `g5` materialises it; reverse emits a spurious `CCX(sum[2][3], carry[2][3] → carry[2][4])` |
| `W = 3`, `{ZERO, ONE, Q}` | `[Q,O,O]` | `[O,O,Z]` | 11 | 13 | **not** I6b — see below |
| `W = 4`, `{ZERO, ONE, Q}` | `[Z,Q,O,O]` | `[O,O,Z,Z]` | 14 | 16 | second mechanism |

Failing-mask counts for the ripple composition (all masks, `3^{2W}` of them): `W=2` → 0, `W=3` → 3, `W=4` → 153. Restricted to `{ZERO, Q}` masks with no constant-`ONE` bits: 0 at `W ≤ 4`, **24 at `W = 5`, 272 at `W = 6`**, and growing. K11 is instantiated at `W ∈ {1,8,16,32,64,128}` (opcode_table.yaml:186) — every non-degenerate width is above the threshold.

**⚠️ A SECOND mechanism exists, and the ordering rule does not cover it.** In the `W = 3` witness above, an audit of every scratch bit finds **zero** control-reads preceding materialisation — I6b-ordering's antecedent is fully satisfied — and the replay is *still* non-cancelling. What happens instead is a **target**-side fold:

```
forward, adder j=1, i=2:  g1 CX(accum[1][2]=ONE -> sum[1][2]=ZERO)  -> flip constant, 0 gates
                          g2 CX(pp[1][2]=ONE    -> sum[1][2]=ONE )  -> flip constant, 0 gates
                          g5 CX(carry[1][2]=Q   -> sum[1][2]=ZERO)  -> MATERIALISE, emit CX (1)
reverse , adder j=1, i=2:  g5 CX(carry[1][2]=Q   -> sum[1][2]=Q   )  -> emit CX (1)   cancels
                          g2 CX(pp[1][2]=ONE    -> sum[1][2]=Q   )  -> emit X  (1)   <== SPURIOUS
                          g1 CX(accum[1][2]=ONE -> sum[1][2]=Q   )  -> emit X  (1)   <== SPURIOUS
```

A `CX` from a `ONE` control into a **constant** target is a zero-gate constant flip; the same `CX` into a **qubit** target is a real `X`. Because kinds are monotone (D6), any scratch bit that is constant-flipped early and materialised later re-emits those flips as physical gates on the reverse pass. So the sound invariant has to bite on *kind changes*, not on control-reads:

> **The generalised condition.** In the compute half, no scratch bit may change kind
> (`ZERO`/`ONE` → `Q`) after it has been used as *either* a control *or* a fold-decided
> target.

**The plan's I6(b) discharges this the blunt way, and that is why it was chosen:** if every
scratch bit is `CQ_BIT_Q` at step 0, *no* scratch bit ever changes kind, so both mechanisms
are dead by construction rather than by per-kernel discipline. Verified on the ripple
composition — with pre-materialisation the exhaustive sweep gives **0 failing masks** at
`W = 2, 3, 4` over the full `3^{2W}` mask space. The Cuccaro composition inherits the
guarantee from the same driver property, and was not separately swept.

**Test consequence — still live under I6(b), and it is the reason to keep this record.**
I6(b) closes the hazard *in the emitter*; it does not by itself prove the emitter got it
right. The regression that catches a lapse in pre-materialisation is an L3 sweep over
**all three bit-kinds**, not a two-valued quantum/classical mask. The `{ZERO, Q}`-only
class first fails at `W = 5`, and Rule 10's L1/L3 exhaustive widths are `W ∈ {1,2,4,8}` —
`W = 4` is clean on that alphabet. It is caught at `W = 4` **only if the mask sweep
includes constant-`ONE` bits** (153 failing masks there), and at `W = 8` on any alphabet.
Per IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md:435, put the named witnesses in the **fixed** mask set: a
regression must not depend on random masks to be caught.

**Provenance of these numbers (Rule 17).** Julia was not run; no Bennett code was executed. These are from a Python model of PRD §3's fold table (rows: `X`/`CX`/`CCX`, materialisation, and the `c1 = ONE → emit_cx(c2,t)` collapse taken symmetrically in `c2`) driven over the literal loop structure transcribed from multiplier.jl:21–31 and adder.jl:8–16. The model is a model: it pins the *emission decisions*, not libcqops' not-yet-written emitter. **UNVERIFIED-BY-EXECUTION**, and additionally **not re-run for the Cuccaro composition**.

**Physical severity.** In every witness inspected the spurious gates are physically self-cancelling on the real state (`X·X = I`; the spurious `CCX` fires on a control the reverse `g5` has already restored to `|0⟩`). The damage is to the **shadow**: the extra materialisation leaves a scratch qubit whose shadow is *unknown*, so `cq_scratch_free` hits a not-provably-zero rail and hard-errors (Rule 6). That is fail-loud, not silent — but pre-I6(b) it meant **every mixed-kind `mul` aborted the compile**. I6(b) lands in Step 8, ahead of Step 12 and well ahead of K11's Step 16.

**The classical short-circuit is not optional.** Pre-materialisation allocates `W² + 2W`
qubits at step 0 *unconditionally once the sandwich is entered*. If every bit of both `a`
and `b` is classical, `cq_mul` must fold to a constant **before** `cq_sandwich` is called
and allocate nothing — otherwise L5's "zero gates and zero qubits fully-classical" breaks
(IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md §0.2 consequence 2). §4 states the resulting two-branch qubit
count. The all-classical case does **not** cost scratch qubits.

Alternatives considered and rejected: reordering `g4`/`g5` (a re-derivation, Rule 1); recording the emitted operand triples and replaying the recorded stream (a gate list inside the library, Rule 13); the ordering-only rule (defeated by the target-side mechanism above).

### Derivation — counting the loops

Let `R = result_width`; libcqops uses `R = W` (§2: CQ_lang's `mul` is same-width).

**Partial products** (multiplier.jl:21–28). Outer `i` runs `1..W` with `shift = i−1`;
inner `k` runs `1..W` but breaks at `dest = k + shift > R` (multiplier.jl:26), so iteration
`i` emits `min(W, R − i + 1)` Toffolis. At `R = W` that is `W − i + 1`, and

  `Σ_{i=1..W} (W − i + 1) = W + (W−1) + … + 1 = W(W+1)/2`.

This term is **unchanged** by the strategy decision — it belongs to the skeleton, not the
adder.

**Inner adder — K8 Cuccaro at width `W`** (adder.jl:64–146). Re-verified against K08.md
§3's `push!`-site table, whose block-by-block derivation I re-counted from the source on
2026-08-14: `2 + 2(W−3) + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2(W−3) + 2 = 4W − 2` CNOT and
`1 + (W−3) + 1 + (W−3) + 1 = 2W − 3` Toffoli, `0` NOT, total `6W − 5`. Valid for `W ≥ 2`
(the `W == 2` branch at adder.jl:84–96 pushes 6 CNOT + 1 Toffoli, and `4·2−2 = 6`,
`2·2−3 = 1`). Cross-checked upstream by `test/test_op6a_cuccaro_gate_count.jl:38–41`,
which asserts exactly these three counts plus `length(gates) == 6W−5` at
`W ∈ {2,3,4,8,16,32,64}`.

**Compute half** = partial products + `W` Cuccaro calls. Every one of the `W` calls emits
the full `6W − 5`, because under I6(b) `accum` is `CQ_BIT_Q` from step 0 and the
zero-accumulator fold that Bennett's `_fold_constants` exploits does not fire:

| | Compute half (per `cq_mul`) |
|---|---|
| `NOT` (X) | `0` |
| `CNOT` (CX) | `W(4W − 2)` = **`4W² − 2W`** |
| `Toffoli` (CCX) | `W(W+1)/2 + W(2W − 3)` = `(W² + W + 4W² − 6W)/2` = **`(5W² − 5W)/2`** |
| **total** | `W(W+1)/2 + W(6W − 5)` = **`(13W² − 9W)/2`** |

Both halves of that Toffoli term are integral: `(5W² − 5W)/2 = 5W(W−1)/2`, and `W(W−1)` is
even at every `W`. Likewise `(13W² − 9W)/2 = W(13W − 9)/2` — if `W` is odd then `13W − 9`
is even.

**Sandwiched total** — what M18 actually emits and what the L4 golden pins — is
`2 ×` compute half `+ W` copy-out `CX` (PRD §5; structurally the same wrap Bennett applies
globally at bennett_transform.jl:344, 346–347, 356–358):

| | **Sandwiched (libcqops M18) — THE PINNED FORMULA** |
|---|---|
| `NOT` (X) | **`0`** |
| `CNOT` (CX) | `2(4W² − 2W) + W` = **`8W² − 3W`** |
| `Toffoli` (CCX) | `2 · (5W² − 5W)/2` = **`5W² − 5W`** |
| **total** | `2 · (13W² − 9W)/2 + W` = **`13W² − 8W`** |

Consistency check: `(8W² − 3W) + (5W² − 5W) = 13W² − 8W` ✔.

### `W = 1` must be pinned separately — the closed form is WRONG there

`lower_add_cuccaro!` delegates to the out-of-place `lower_add!` at `W ≤ 1`
(adder.jl:66) and is no longer an accumulator; upstream's own test says so
(`test_op6a_cuccaro_gate_count.jl:45–56`: "the formulas above don't apply at W=1"). K08.md
D1 accordingly pins the `W = 1` accumulator as libcqops' own one-liner,
`(X, CX, CCX) = (0, 1, 0)`.

**The trap:** at `W = 1` the sandwiched closed form gives total `13 − 8 = 5`, and the
uniform path really does emit 5 gates — but the per-type split is wrong. Evaluating the
closed form gives `CX = 8 − 3 = 5`, `CCX = 5 − 5 = 0`; the uniform path actually emits
1 phase-P `CCX` + 1 accumulator `CX` per half, plus 1 copy-out `CX`, i.e.
`(X, CX, CCX) = (0, 3, 2)`. **Pinning the closed form at `W = 1` would pin a wrong golden
that happens to have the right total** — exactly the failure mode K08.md D1 warns about.

**Recommended, and it must be recorded in the plan rather than settled by whichever
implementation lands first:** `mul` at `i1` is `dst ^= a·b = dst ^= a & b`, which is **K2
(bitwise AND) with one `CCX`** — a *clean* kernel with no scratch, no sandwich, no
accumulator. That is a delegation to an existing catalogue entry, not a new construction,
so Rule 1 is satisfied. Pin `W = 1` as **`(X, CX, CCX) = (0, 0, 1)`, total 1, scratch 0**,
and record the uniform-path alternative `(0, 3, 2)` / 5 gates / 2 qubits as rejected.

### What I6(b) does and does not make W-only — read before pinning the golden

Under I6(b) **no fold on a scratch bit can fire**, so the entire accumulator contribution
— `4W² − 2W` CX and `2W² − 3W` CCX in the compute half — is a function of `W` alone. That
is the part that used to make goldens fragile, because it depended on data flow *inside*
the kernel, and it is now gone. Risk **R5** is retired for this kernel.

**One residual dependence survives, and it is on the source side, not the scratch side.**
Phase-P gates are `CCX(a[k], b[j] → pp[j][d])`: the target is scratch (always `CQ_BIT_Q`),
but the two controls are the kernel's **operands**, whose kinds I6(b) does not touch. The
fold table still dispatches on them:

| `a[k]` | `b[j]` | emitted |
|---|---|---|
| either is `BIT_ZERO` | | nothing |
| `BIT_ONE` | `BIT_ONE` | 1 `X` on `pp[j][d]` (a real `X` — the target is a qubit) |
| `BIT_ONE` | `CQ_BIT_Q` (or vice versa) | 1 `CX` |
| `CQ_BIT_Q` | `CQ_BIT_Q` | 1 `CCX` |

So the *step count* is fixed at `n_compute = (13W² − 9W)/2` regardless of mask, and the
*gate count* equals it exactly when every operand bit is `CQ_BIT_Q`. Two consequences:

1. **Soundness is unaffected.** Operand kinds are immutable across the compute half
   (I6(a): sources appear only as controls, controls are never materialised), so forward
   and reverse still emit identically for *every* mask. The sandwich cancels either way.
2. **Pin the L4 golden at the all-quantum operand mask**, and say so in the golden's
   header comment alongside the Bennett commit (risk R3). The table above is that mask.
   A golden taken at a mixed mask would be smaller and would not be reproducible from the
   formula.

**Deliberately forgone saving.** Bennett's `_fold_constants` pass exploits the
zero-initialised accumulator — `test/test_gate_count_regression.jl:97–99` records `x*x`
Toffoli dropping from `296 / 1232` (pre-fix) to `144 / 664` (post-fix) at i8/i16, i.e.
roughly a 2× saving. libcqops has no folding pass, and under I6(b) PRD §3's emission-time
fold table cannot reproduce it either, because `accum` and the permanently-zero
`pp[j][0..j−1]` are qubits from step 0. **This is the price of a `W`-only golden and it is
paid knowingly.** Do not "recover" it by relaxing pre-materialisation.

### Upstream reconciliation → **Appendix A**

K11's pinned construction has **no upstream artifact to reconcile against**: "shift-add
over Cuccaro" exists nowhere in Bennett, is called by no Bennett code path, and is
exercised by no Bennett test. Its golden is self-pinned. That is the accepted cost of the
strategy decision (§2, §5 delta 4), and it is why the **ripple** figures are retained in
**Appendix A**, where they reconcile against BENCHMARKS.md:14–15 with **residual zero on
all six columns at two widths**. Appendix A validates the skeleton and the wrap
arithmetic; it does not validate K11's numbers.

### The trade, stated once

Against the withdrawn ripple headline, Cuccaro buys `2W` fewer Toffolis and a **2.85×
smaller scratch** (3104 → 1088 qubits at `W = 32`), and pays `2W² − 6W` more total gates —
almost all of them `CNOT`, which is the cheap gate under a surface-code cost model. On the
T-count-and-qubits metric this project actually optimises for, Cuccaro dominates. The
countervailing cost is not a gate count at all: it is the loss of the upstream
cross-check (Appendix A).

## 4. Ancilla count

**Pinned: `W² + 2W` scratch qubits, all drawn at step 0.**

| Register | Count | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `accum` | `W` | ONE register, updated in place across all `W` iterations — this is the whole point of the Cuccaro substitution. Ripple needs a fresh `accum` per iteration (multiplier.jl:15 + the rebind at multiplier.jl:30). |
| `pp[j]`, `j = 0..W−1` | `W · W` | multiplier.jl:23. Cannot be recycled: Cuccaro *restores* its addend (adder.jl:50–51), so `pp[j]` still holds the partial product after the call. |
| `x[j]`, `j = 0..W−1` | `W` | adder.jl:69, `X = allocate!(wa, 1)` — one per Cuccaro call. K08.md §4: exactly 1 ancilla for every `W ≥ 2`, independent of `W`. **Owned and passed in by K11**, not allocated inside K8 — see §2b's interface note; a mid-compute-half allocation would break I6(b). |
| **total** | **`W² + 2W`** | `1024 + 64 = 1088` at `W = 32`; `64 + 16 = 80` at `W = 8`. |

**Every one of these is a qubit, not a maybe-qubit.** Under I6(b) `cq_sandwich`
pre-materialises the whole region at step 0. Three things follow, and all three are easy
to get wrong:

1. **Step 0 costs QUBITS, never GATES.** Scratch is born `CQ_BIT_ZERO` and
   `cq_materialise` emits an `X` only when the constant was `1`. So pre-materialisation
   draws `W² + 2W` qubits from the pool and emits **zero** gates. Do not add `X` gates for
   it, and do not let a "materialisation cost" appear in the L4 golden.
2. **The count no longer depends on the operand mask.** Pre-decision, a mixed-kind
   multiply would leave some scratch bits classical and the qubit count would float. It
   does not any more: `W² + 2W` at every mask that enters the sandwich.
3. **…except the mask that does not enter the sandwich at all.** If every bit of both `a`
   and `b` is classical, `cq_mul` folds to a constant **before** `cq_sandwich` is reached
   and allocates **0 qubits and 0 gates** (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md §0.2 consequence 2;
   Rule 10 L5). The all-classical case is **not** `W² + 2W`. It is zero.

They come from one place — `cq_scratch_alloc` drawing from the LIFO pool (I3 guarantees each is `|0⟩`) — and they are **all** returned to `|0⟩` by the sandwich's reverse half, then released by `cq_scratch_free`, which asserts each is provably zero (Rule 6). None of them can be freed early: every one is still needed as a control by the reverse replay.

**The Cuccaro ancillae are freed by the sandwich, not by K8.** K08.md §4 records that
libcqops' two-bit shadow *cannot prove* `x = |0⟩` on exit from a standalone K8 call — the
shadow forgets correlations and poison is sticky — so a bare K8 would have to leak the
ancilla or carry a hand-justified shadow override. **Inside K11 that problem does not
arise:** `x[j]` is scratch, and the sandwich driver re-establishes known-zero
*structurally* by replaying the compute half in reverse under I6. This is a concrete
argument for K8 having a sandwiched consumer rather than a bare one.

**One qubit-count option deliberately not taken.** All `W` Cuccaro ancillae are `|0⟩`
before and after each call (adder.jl:50–54, pinned upstream by

## 5. Deltas from upstream

1. **Bennett-in-the-small (the sandwich).** Bennett relies on the single global wrap at bennett_transform.jl:344 (`2 * length(lr.gates) + length(lr.output_wires)`), 346–347 (forward + copy-out) and 356–358 (reverse); libcqops wraps K11 locally per PRD §5. Structurally the two are the *same* transform (`2 × compute + W` copy-out `CX`), which is why Appendix A's reconciliation is exact. Cost: 2× the compute half, non-negotiable (Rule 2).

2. **`WireAllocator` → LIFO pool.** `lower_mul_wide!` never calls `free!` (wire_allocator.jl:39), so Bennett's wire numbering is monotone and reuse never happens inside the multiplier. libcqops' pool is LIFO (D4) and reclaims on `cq_scratch_free`. This changes qubit *indices* in the trace but not counts — which is exactly why kernel goldens pin **counts, not traces** (risk R5).

3. **Constant folding happens at emission, not as a pass — and inside the sandwich it is switched OFF by construction.** Bennett's `_fold_constants` is an optional post-pass, defaulted `true` (src/Bennett.jl:146, src/lowering/driver.jl:105, applied at driver.jl:375–376); libcqops has no pass, and PRD §3's fold table would achieve the same short-circuit inline. **Under I6(b) it cannot, for scratch.** Every scratch bit is `CQ_BIT_Q` from step 0, so the zero-accumulator saving Bennett gets (`x*x` Toffoli `296 → 144` at i8, `1232 → 664` at i16 — test_gate_count_regression.jl:97–99) is deliberately forgone. Net effect versus the pre-decision reading: libcqops' K11 counts are **no longer `≤` the closed form and kind-dependent on scratch**; they are the closed form, with the single source-side residual documented at the end of §3.

4. **⚠️ THE HEADLINE DELTA — the inner adder is Cuccaro, and Bennett's is ripple.**
   `multiplier.jl:29` calls **`lower_add!`**, the out-of-place ripple adder; the string
   `cuccaro` does not appear in `multiplier.jl` at all, and no `lower_mul_*` anywhere in
   the pinned tree composes shift-add with Cuccaro. **libcqops substitutes
   `lower_add_cuccaro!` into that slot. This is a derived composition, not a port.**

5. **⚠️ NEW 2026-08-14 — we PRE-MATERIALISE the scratch region; Bennett does not, and cannot.**
   `cq_sandwich` turns every bit of `accum`, `pp[0..W−1]` and `x[0..W−1]` into a
   `CQ_BIT_Q` qubit at step 0, before the first compute step runs (I6(b),
   IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md §0.2). Bennett has no equivalent step and no need for one.
   **The question cannot even arise upstream, because Bennett has no classical operand
   kind at all:** `src/gates.jl:1` defines `const WireIndex = Int`, and every field of
   every gate — `NOTGate.target`, `CNOTGate.control/target`,
   `ToffoliGate.control1/control2/target` (gates.jl:6–22) — is a `WireIndex`. A Bennett
   gate operand is *always* a wire. There is no `BIT_ZERO`/`BIT_ONE`/`BIT_Q` trichotomy,
   so there is no fold table, so no gate's expansion can depend on when a wire acquired
   its value, so replay-in-reverse is unconditionally sound upstream. Bennett folds
   constants in a *separate optional IR pass* (`_fold_constants`, driver.jl:375–376) that
   runs before the wrap and therefore folds both halves identically.

   libcqops' tri-valued bit (I1) is what creates the hazard and pre-materialisation is
   what removes it. Consequences, all of which are visible in §3 and §4 above:
   - **Gates: none.** Scratch is born `BIT_ZERO`, and `cq_materialise` emits an `X` only
     for a `1` constant. Step 0 is free in gates and costs `W² + 2W` qubits.
   - **Counts rise relative to a folding emitter** — the forgone zero-accumulator saving,
     quantified in delta 3 — and in exchange become a function of `W` alone on the whole
     scratch side. That retires risk **R5** for this kernel.
   - **Qubits rise relative to a folding emitter**: scratch bits that would have stayed
     classical (notably the permanently-zero `pp[j][0..j−1]`) each take a pool qubit.
   - **The all-classical operand case is exempt and must be**, because it never enters
     `cq_sandwich`: `cq_mul` folds to a constant first, 0 gates and 0 qubits (Rule 10 L5).
