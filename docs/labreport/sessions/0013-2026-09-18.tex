\field{The ask}
Whether v2 --- floating point --- can start now, on the premise that Bennett.jl provides every
construction and every classical routine is \texttt{X}/\texttt{CX}/\texttt{CCX}, ``so we just copy what
Bennett emits''; then, in turn, why \texttt{fadd} needs $\sim$30k qubits, whether the integer
additions inside it are the kernels we already ship, what \texttt{cq\_sandwich} is, whether the
step functions are the existing operations, which f64 operations are in (``also \texttt{sqrt}?''),
why not the transcendentals, and finally: write the docs and file the issues.

\field{Not taken}
\emph{Copying an emitted gate stream} --- there is none in the snapshot, and Rule 13 forbids
holding one. \emph{The transcendentals as kernels} --- CQ\_lang's Rule 18 source-injects
\texttt{math.h} bodies, so no symbol exists to implement; they arrive through the arithmetic port
at CQ\_lang's algorithm, and a Bennett-backed single opcode is a CQ\_lang table row, filed there
if ever wanted (\S7.8). \emph{The host \texttt{double} operator as the classical short-circuit}
--- measured x86-specific on the IEEE-unspecified cells; ARM disagrees, so classical and quantum
mode would diverge per host (\S7.4). \emph{CSE and field narrowing in the transcription} --- D9's
K12 refusal, K11's optimisation-shaped mutant (\S7.2). \emph{\texttt{lrint}/\texttt{llrint},
\texttt{fpext}/\texttt{fptrunc}, \texttt{frem} at f64} --- liveness (\S7.9). \emph{Committing} ---
