\field{The ask}
Which of the open beads belong to v1, and whether closing them would complete the
integer surface --- then, on the answer, a decision on \bead{3ep}: whether the 24
\texttt{bitcast} symbols stay in the fp abort bucket.

\field{What landed}
No code. \bead{3ep} is resolved in the two documents that own it: PRD \S15 D16 gains a
third worked example, and \texttt{docs/cqrt\_census.txt} gains a cross-reference under the
\textsc{domain split} line, which is where the name rule (\emph{a cast is ``purely
integer'' iff both endpoints are integer}) is written down exactly once. The disposition
does not move --- the 24 keep \texttt{``fp is v2''} and the $884/1595$ partition is
unchanged --- so no count in either document changes.

The finding worth carrying is the \emph{ground}, because the plausible one is false. The
bitcasts look like D14's \texttt{\_inv} family, so the reflex argument is ``defer them,
nothing emits them''. Measured, they are among the most heavily emitted symbols in the
2479-symbol grid. What settles it instead is D23's own test --- \emph{are there fixtures
otherwise in scope that stop only on this abort?} --- applied to this family and coming out
the opposite way from tape: not one of the 127 bitcast fixtures is otherwise fp-clean, so
implementing the 24 unblocks zero fixtures. \texttt{bd remember
bitcast-deferral-is-caller-reachability-not-non-emission}.

\field{Not taken}
Implementing the 24 on \bead{3ep}'s own capability argument, which is correct as far as it
goes and was upheld: they genuinely need no floating-point capability. It buys nothing ---
every caller needs fp anyway --- and it would carve a per-symbol exception into a grid M27
expands mechanically, which is the forked-shim hazard. The 12 live symbols become reachable
exactly when fp does.

Also not taken: rewriting the name rule itself. The two readings of ``fp-touching'' do come
apart, and the entry records that they do rather than collapsing them.
